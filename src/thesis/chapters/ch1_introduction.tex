\section{Presentation of Project}

\subsection{Project Overview}

\hs This project aims to develop a robust and deployable deep learning framework for the automatic detection and pixel-level segmentation of cracks in large-scale, high-resolution aerial imagery captured by UAVs. The core challenge lies in overcoming the limitations of current models, which struggle to balance the detection of fine, thread-like cracks (often as thin as 1-3 pixels) with maintaining the global context of massive images (up to >50 megapixels).

To achieve this, the project will conduct empirical experiments using a range of advanced models, \hl{including semantic segmentation architectures (U-Net, DeepLabV3+), instance segmentation models (YOLO), and transformer-based models (SegFormer, Swin Transformer)}, to identify the most effective approach. A key innovation of the project is the design and implementation of a tile-based inference pipeline with blended overlaps, which will enable the seamless processing of arbitrarily large images on a single GPU. To ensure robustness and real-world applicability, the model will be trained and validated on a diverse collection of public crack datasets and further fine-tuned on a custom dataset of high-resolution UAV images. \hl{The system's performance will be rigorously assessed using standard segmentation metrics like IoU, Precision, Recall, and F1-Score}, with a final goal of creating an accurate and efficient tool for routine infrastructure inspection that can contribute to improved public safety and proactive maintenance.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

\hs \hl{Routine inspection of critical infrastructure, including bridges, buildings, and runways, is essential for public safety. However, current manual inspection methods are expensive (averaging \$X per mile), subjective, and limited in coverage. While AI-based crack detection has shown promise on close-up images (typically $< 5$ megapixels), applying these methods to aerial imagery introduces severe challenges. When a UAV captures a building facade from 15m altitude, a structural crack just 2mm wide is resolved as only 1--3 pixels in the 100+ megapixel image-occupying less than 0.01\% of the total area. Furthermore, the massive image size exceeds the memory limits of standard GPUs (typically 8--24GB VRAM), requiring specialized inference strategies. Current models fail to balance the preservation of these fine spatial details with global contextual understanding, and lack efficient gigapixel-scale inference pipelines.}

\subsection{Objectives}

\hs Build an accurate, precise, and practically deployable AI model to detect and segment cracks presenting on large buildings, contributing to reduced infrastructural improvement, early maintenance and public safety. Furthermore, this project aims to design and validate a deep learning framework for automatic, pixel-level crack segmentation in high-resolution aerial imagery. Specific goals are to:

\begin{itemize}        
    \item Develop an empirical experiment on segmentation and detection models using modified Semantic, Segmentation Models, Instance Segmentation models, and transformer-based models that are capable of capturing fine cracks on high-resolution images.
    \item Implement a tile-based inference pipeline with blended overlaps, allowing seamless processing of arbitrarily large-scale, high-resolution images on a single GPU.
    \item Evaluate the system on multiple real-world aerial crack datasets, comparing against published baselines.
    \item Conduct a comprehensive performance analysis using standard segmentation metrics such as Intersection over Union (IoU), Precision, Recall, and F1-Score to quantify the model's effectiveness in detecting and segmenting cracks.
\end{itemize}
